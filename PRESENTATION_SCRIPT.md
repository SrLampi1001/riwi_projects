# Presentation Script

## Scene 1: Client Meets the Business Owner

Client:
Good morning. Thank you for meeting with me today. I own a local library, and lately it has become difficult to manage all the books we have. We still use paper and Excel files, and it's very inefficient.

Business Owner:
Good morning! I'm glad you came to us. Could you please explain what exactly you need and what problems you are facing?

Client:
Of course. I need an application that allows us to store information about all the books in the library, such as title, author, category, and quantity in stock.
Additionally, we also sell some books, so I would like the system to manage sales and update the stock automatically.

Business Owner:
That sounds like a great project. Do you also need user accounts or reports?

Client:
Yes, ideally the librarians should be able to log in, add or remove books, and view sales reports. The system should be easy to use because not everyone is very technical.

Business Owner:
Understood. This is definitely something my team can build. I'll discuss it with them and get back to you with a solution.

Client:
Perfect. I'm looking forward to it.

## Scene 2: Business Owner Meets the Team

Business Owner:
Hello team, I have a new project for us. A client needs a library management application. It should track books in stock and also allow selling books.

SCRUM Master:
That sounds manageable. Let's clarify the main requirements first. What are the core features?

Business Owner:
Book inventory management, sales functionality, user login for librarians, and basic reports.

SCRUM Master:
Great. We can organize this into sprints. First sprint for inventory management, second for sales, and third for reports and polishing.

Developer:
That works for me. I can design a database for books, users, and sales. The interface will be simple and user-friendly.

SCRUM Master:
Perfect. I'll manage the sprint planning and make sure we stay on schedule.

Business Owner:
Excellent. Let's proceed. The client wants something reliable and easy to use.

## Scene 3: Development Phase (Short Internal Conversation)

SCRUM Master:
We're halfway through the sprint. How is the development going?

Developer:
Very well. The inventory system is finished. You can add, edit, delete, and search books.
Sales functionality is also implemented, and stock updates automatically when a book is sold.

SCRUM Master:
Great work. Any issues?

Developer:
No major issues. I just added a dashboard with reports for total sales and available stock.

SCRUM Master:
Perfect. Let's prepare for the client presentation.

## Scene 4: Presenting the Application to the Client

Business Owner:
Welcome back! We're happy to present the library application we developed for you.

Client:
Great! I'm excited to see it.

Developer:
Let me walk you through it.
This is the main dashboard. From here, librarians can log in securely.
In the inventory section, you can add new books, update quantities, and see which books are out of stock.

Client:
That already looks very clear and simple.

Developer:
Here is the sales section. When a book is sold, the system records the transaction and automatically reduces the stock.
You can also generate daily or monthly sales reports.

SCRUM Master:
We developed the application using an agile approach, so in the future we can easily add new features if you need them.

Client:
That's excellent. This solves all the problems we discussed.

## Scene 5: Closing the Deal

Client:
I'm very satisfied with the result. This application will save us a lot of time and reduce mistakes.

Business Owner:
We're very happy to hear that. If you need maintenance or new features, our team will be available.

Client:
Perfect. Here is the payment as agreed. Thank you for your professionalism.

Business Owner:
Thank you for your trust. It was a pleasure working with you.

SCRUM Master & Developer:
Thank you!
